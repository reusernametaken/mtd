What is MeshCore?

MeshCore is the radio protocol Mesh That Divide runs on: long-range, low-power LoRa radios that talk directly to each other and hop messages hand to hand across a chain of nodes, no cell tower and no internet connection required anywhere in the path.

Each node — a small radio, sometimes solar-powered, sometimes just plugged into a wall — either talks straight to another node in range or repeats a message along toward one that's further out. String enough of them across a ridgeline and the network survives exactly the conditions that take down everything else: no power grid, no fiber, no cell service.

It's licence-free to use — unlicensed ISM-band radio, the same category as a garage door opener or a baby monitor. Anyone can carry a node. Anyone can help extend the chain.

Why MeshCore today?

MeshCore and Meshtastic both run on the same class of LoRa hardware. This network runs on MeshCore today, because it's what its own nodes and the wider Colorado mesh community around it use. Other protocols may be added later.

Who's behind this

One person in Kremmling, deciding a community-owned mesh network was worth building even if it began as a single node on a single rooftop. It's grown as far as neighbors, hikers and other mesh-curious people have been willing to pitch in a radio, a mounting point, or an afternoon.

No investors, no ad revenue, no data being sold anywhere. If you've got a rooftop with line of sight to any of the repeater hops on the map, or you're already running MeshCore gear somewhere in Grand County, you're already part of who's meshing that divide.
