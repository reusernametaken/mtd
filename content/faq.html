...MeshCore?

MeshCore is the radio protocol Mesh That Divide runs on: long-range, low-power LoRa radios that talk directly to each other and hop messages hand to hand across a chain of nodes, no cell tower and no internet connection required anywhere in the path.

Each node -- a small radio, sometimes solar-powered, sometimes just plugged into a wall -- either talks straight to another node in range or repeats a message along toward one that's further out. String enough of them across a ridgeline and the network survives exactly the conditions that take down everything else: no power grid, no fiber, no cell service.

It's licence-free to use -- unlicensed ISM-band radio, the same category as a garage door opener or a baby monitor. Anyone can carry a node. Anyone can help extend the chain.

Why not MeshTastic?

MeshCore and Meshtastic both run on the same class of LoRa hardware, but this project is standardizing on MeshCore going forward -- it's what our own nodes and the wider Colorado mesh community around us are actually converging on. One protocol, one connected network, instead of two incompatible ones splitting the same handful of volunteers.

FAQ

Who is it for?

Anybody -- explorers/recluses, loudmouths/lurkers, daredevils/safety enthusiasts -- you name it.

What is it for?

Getting in tune, and keeping in touch. These low band, low power radios pair with our cell phones and let us text one another without any cellular service at all.

When is it for?

Whether you're often active and need contact, kicking back and socializing, or there's an emergency and your phone suddenly has no bars; LoRa radios with MeshCore deliver accessible live messaging connectivity that will always belong to us.

Where is it for?

It's for places that cellular service can't always go, or for knowing there's a backup line in the safety of your home. There's no infrastructure to crash -- the radios ARE the mesh -- on the road, the trail, or even the river, this network exists where those who wish to use it show up and switch it on.

Why is it for?

Your questions sure are getting funny, but it's for why we have some space to look out for ourselves. We have access to affordable technologies now that can let us build our own autonomous communication systems with no reliance on existing infrastructures -- whether that's because we object to their paywalls, we question their security, reliability, or flexibility, OR, we just plain feel like it!
